## Is Once Upon A Katamari Worth It? The Short Answer

If you've ever rolled an enormous ball of household clutter through a cartoon city, you already know this series runs on pure chaos and charm. So the question hanging over the newest entry is simple: is Once Upon A Katamari worth it? For most longtime fans, the answer is yes — and Once Upon A Katamari is worth it mainly because of how much content it crams into a single package, even if it never reinvents the formula.

Reviews land in a similar place. Outlets that went hands-on describe it as a genuinely funny, whimsical, silly game, with at least one major review scoring it a 4/5. Others are more measured: still really fun, but not quite the dream version of a new Katamari game some players had pictured. That gap between "very good" and "revolutionary" is exactly what makes the value question worth digging into.

Here's the fast version:

| Player type | Worth it? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Longtime Katamari fan | Yes | Familiar loop, polished, packed with stages |
| Series newcomer | Yes | Easy to learn, no prior games required |
| Co-op or party player | Depends | Multiplayer is described as fine, not essential |
| Completionist and score chaser | Yes | Challenges and customization add hours |
| Player wanting a full reinvention | Not really | It refines the formula rather than replacing it |

The takeaway: this is a comfort-food sequel, not a reinvention. If that sounds appealing, keep reading — the rest of this breakdown covers exactly what you get for your money.

## What You Actually Get: Levels, Challenges, and Customization

The core pitch hasn't changed since the original Katamari Damacy: roll a sticky ball over objects smaller than itself, watch it grow, and eventually swallow entire skylines. What has changed is the volume of stuff wrapped around that loop.

Multiple reviewers highlight the sheer number of levels, the challenge structure, and the customization options as the strongest argument for buying in. In other words, the value isn't in one spectacular set piece — it's in the accumulation.

| Content type | What to expect | Value impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main stages | Classic size-based rolling objectives | High — the backbone of the game |
| Challenges | Timed, size-gated, or themed variants | High — adds replay pressure |
| Customization | Cosmetics and personal touches for your run | Medium to high — depends on your taste |
| Multiplayer modes | Competitive and cooperative options | Medium — "alright" per reviews |
| Score chasing | Leaderboard-style repeat play | High for perfectionists |

### Levels and the core loop

Stages escalate the way you'd hope: you start small, awkward, and slightly pathetic, then finish by hoovering up buildings. The pacing is the series' signature trick, and it still works. Several reviews specifically call out the number of levels as a reason this entry feels generous rather than thin.

### Challenges

Challenge variants are where the "one more run" impulse lives. They remix familiar stages with constraints, which is a cheap way to double your content without building new worlds — but it works, and it's a big part of why the package feels full.

### Customization

Customization options let you stamp some personality onto your rolling chaos. Reviews describe these as a meaningful addition rather than an afterthought, though how much you care will depend on whether you're the type to fiddle with cosmetics at all.

## Multiplayer, Replay Value, and Platform Notes

Multiplayer exists, and reviews describe it as fine — functional, occasionally fun, rarely the reason you'd buy the game. If you have a friend who's equally into the series, it's a nice bonus. If you're buying *for* the multiplayer, temper your expectations.

Replay value, on the other hand, is genuinely strong. Between challenge modes, score chasing, and the natural urge to replay a stage "just once more" to hit a bigger size, this is a game that rewards a second and third pass through familiar content.

### Performance on Switch and Steam Deck

Handheld play suits this series almost perfectly — short stages, bite-sized sessions, and a low-stakes vibe. One Steam Deck-focused review called it a joy to play on the handheld, and described it as exactly the continuation the series needed.

| Platform | Reported experience | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nintendo Switch | Solid handheld and docked play | Commuters and couch players |
| Steam Deck / PC | Described as a joy on handheld | Players who want flexibility |

## Where Once Upon A Katamari Stumbles

No game is perfect, and this one has a few honest complaints. One review noted quality-of-life improvements alongside additions that some players wished would simply go away — a reminder that not every modern convenience lands well in a deliberately weird series.

The bigger critique is philosophical. One reviewer summed it up as still really fun, but not the dream new Katamari game they'd imagined. If you were hoping for a bold reinvention, you'll feel that gap. If you wanted more of what you already love, you won't.

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| --- | --- |
| Funny, whimsical tone that never takes itself seriously | Formula feels familiar rather than fresh |
| Large number of levels and challenges | Multiplayer is serviceable, not standout |
| Customization adds personal flavor | A few new features divide players |
| Excellent fit for handheld play | Not a dramatic leap for the series |

A useful rule of thumb: judge this as a sequel, not a reboot. Sequels are allowed to be comfortable.

## Tips to Get the Most Value Out of Once Upon A Katamari

If you're still on the fence about whether Once Upon A Katamari is worth it, the smartest move is to squeeze more out of each session. These habits stretch a good game into a great purchase.

| Tip | Why it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Finish each stage at least twice | Your second run is almost always bigger |
| Chase size thresholds before speed | Bigger rolls unlock more of the level |
| Rotate between story and challenge modes | Prevents burnout on repeated stages |
| Play in short handheld sessions | The stage structure suits 10–20 minute bursts |
| Try multiplayer with a patient friend | Best enjoyed casually, not competitively |
| Leave score chasing for last | It's the deepest well of replay value |

A few extra habits worth building:

- **Learn object density, not object lists.** Every stage has clusters of high-value junk. Rolling through a dense cluster beats hunting for one big item.
- **Don't fight the timer.** Rushing early usually means a smaller final size. Speed comes after you know the layout.
- **Revisit old stages after unlocking new ones.** Familiar maps often hide routes you couldn't reach before.
- **Treat the silliness as a feature.** The humor and absurdity are the point — leaning into them is what makes the loop last.

Community reports consistently suggest that players who treat the game as a score-chasing sandbox get far more mileage than those who sprint to the credits.

## FAQ: Your Once Upon A Katamari Worth It Questions

**Is Once Upon A Katamari worth it for someone who has never played the series?**
Yes. The premise is explained through play, not lore, so newcomers can jump straight in. The only real risk is that the deliberate weirdness isn't your flavor of fun.

**How long does the game last?**
There's no single number, because it depends entirely on how much you engage with challenges and score chasing. Player experience suggests the main stages can be rolled through in a weekend, while completionist play extends well beyond that.

**Is the multiplayer strong enough to justify buying it for co-op?**
Probably not on its own. Reviews describe the multiplayer modes as decent rather than exceptional. Buy it for the solo content, and treat multiplayer as a bonus.

**Should I wait for a sale?**
If you're a series fan, the content volume makes it easy to recommend at full price. If you're a newcomer who's unsure about the humor, waiting for a discount is a reasonable hedge — nothing about the game depends on playing it at launch.

**The final verdict:** for fans of the series and for anyone who enjoys a low-pressure, high-charm arcade game, Once Upon A Katamari is worth it. For players who need every sequel to reinvent the wheel, it's an easy pass.
