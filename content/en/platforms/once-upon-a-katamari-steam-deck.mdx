## Performance: Frame Rate, Frame Pacing, and Load Times

The headline number is simple: the game locks to 60 FPS, and the Steam Deck holds that target. According to the SteamDeckHQ review, players should expect a solid 60 FPS throughout rather than a fluctuating frame rate that dips during the biggest katamari rolls.

That matters more here than in most games. Katamari is a game about momentum — you're steering a growing ball through dense, object-filled environments, and every dropped frame makes the camera feel mushy when you're threading between cars and buildings. A locked 60 FPS keeps the roll feeling physical, which is exactly the sensation the series is built on.

| Scenario | What to Expect |
|---|---|
| Early, small katamari stages | Locked 60 FPS, no visible dips |
| Large, cluttered late-game stages | Still 60 FPS, frame pacing holds |
| Cutscenes and story transitions | Smooth, no stutter reported |
| Loading between stages | Short, with no long shader compilation wait |
| Sleep/resume mid-stage | Works, though save-and-quit is safer |

A couple of practical notes. First, because the game is locked rather than uncapped, you won't gain anything by pushing the TDP higher — you're burning battery for frames the game won't produce. Second, if you do see an unexpected hitch, it's far more likely to be a background download or a shader cache rebuild than the game itself struggling.

## Best Steam Deck Settings for Once Upon A Katamari

Because the game is Verified and frame-locked, the best configuration is a conservative one. The goal isn't maximum performance — it's maximum battery life while preserving the 60 FPS lock. Here's a sensible starting point that matches how the game behaves out of the box.

| Setting | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Frame rate limit | 60 FPS | Matches the game's internal lock |
| TDP limit | Leave at default first, then lower gradually | Test how low you can go before dips appear |
| GPU clock | Default / auto | No headroom needed on a locked title |
| Half-rate shading | Off | Not needed; the game isn't GPU-bound |
| FSR | Off | Native resolution looks sharper |
| In-game quality preset | Default | Verified profile is already tuned |
| Vibration | On | Adds weight to collisions and rolls |

The most useful lever is the TDP limit. Start at the default, play a busy stage, then step it down in small increments and replay the same stage. You'll usually find a sweet spot where the frame rate stays locked but the fan noise drops noticeably. Undervolting the APU, if you're comfortable with it, compounds the gain.

Avoid aggressive use of half-rate shading or heavy FSR scaling. On a title that's already hitting its cap, those tools cost image clarity without buying you anything back.

The OLED model is the better handheld for this particular game. The art style is loud, saturated, and full of small details, and the OLED panel's contrast makes the object-packed stages read much more clearly in bright environments.

## Controls, Comfort, and Handheld Tips

Katamari has always been a twin-stick series, and the Steam Deck's layout is a near-perfect match — arguably better than a traditional console pad for long sessions because your hands rest further apart.

| Control Task | How It Feels on Deck |
|---|---|
| Moving the katamari | Left stick, precise and comfortable |
| Camera / dash | Right stick, responsive |
| Jump and dash | Shoulder buttons, easy to reach |
| Menus and catalogue | Touchscreen shortcuts are a bonus |
| Long sessions | Grips reduce fatigue vs. a standard pad |

A few tips that make a real difference:

- **Use gyro for fine camera work.** Enabling gyro on the right stick as a "camera fine-tune" lets you nudge the view without fighting the stick. It's optional, but it helps in tight interiors.
- **Set a rear paddle to jump.** Mapping the jump action to a back button keeps your thumbs on the sticks during tricky rolls.
- **Cap your session with sleep mode.** The game handles sleep/resume reasonably well, but finish a stage before suspending to avoid losing progress.
- **Turn the brightness down a notch.** The game's palette is bright, so you can usually drop brightness and gain battery life without hurting visibility.
- **Play with headphones.** The soundtrack is a huge part of the Katamari experience, and the Deck's speakers undersell it.
