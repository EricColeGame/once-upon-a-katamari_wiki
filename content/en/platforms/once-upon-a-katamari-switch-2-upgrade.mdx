### Mouse Controls: A New Way to Roll

The marquee addition is mouse control. The Switch 2's controllers can be used in a mouse-like fashion, and the developers have wired that directly into the rolling mechanics. Instead of nudging an analog stick, you flick and sweep to change direction, which gives you finer control over small corrections — exactly the kind of precision that matters when you're threading between two obstacles to grab a last-second item.

Community reports from players who have tried the new scheme describe it as the most significant control change the series has seen, though several note that it takes a session or two to unlearn stick habits. If you've been rolling with a controller for years, expect a short adjustment period rather than instant mastery.

### Smoother Framerate and Cleaner Visuals

The performance side of the Once Upon A Katamari Switch 2 upgrade is less flashy than mouse controls but arguably more impactful over a long play session. The enhanced graphics work alongside that: cleaner edges, better definition on small objects, and a generally sharper picture. It's not a remake, and it isn't trying to be — it's the same art direction, just presented with more clarity.

## Tips for Getting the Most Out of the Upgrade

Upgrading is the easy part. Playing well with the new setup takes a little intention. These habits will get you comfortable faster.

1. **Spend your first ten minutes in a low-stakes stage.** Don't chase a high score immediately — just roll around and get used to mouse-style steering.
2. **Switch control schemes mid-session.** Toggling between stick and mouse control helps you feel what each one is good at rather than treating one as strictly better.
3. **Use mouse control for precision, stick control for sweeping turns.** That split plays to the strengths of both inputs.
4. **Replay an old favorite stage first.** Familiar layouts let you isolate the control change from the content change.
5. **Turn the music up.** With a new soundtrack in the mix, it's worth hearing properly.

| Control Style | Best For | Learning Curve |
|---|---|---|
| Analog stick | Wide sweeping turns, relaxed play | None if you've played before |
| Mouse-style | Tight corrections, precision grabs | A session or two to adjust |
| Mixed approach | Optimizing routes and scores | Moderate — requires practice |

One more thing worth doing: revisit a stage that used to frustrate you. The smoother framerate tends to make previously annoying moments — camera snags in cluttered areas, unclear object boundaries — considerably more manageable. It's a quiet improvement, but it changes how the game feels moment to moment.

## FAQ About the Once Upon A Katamari Switch 2 Upgrade

**Is the Once Upon A Katamari Switch 2 upgrade free?**

Yes. The upgrade path is free for players who already own the game on the same platform and account. You claim the upgrade through your console's store rather than buying the game again. New buyers simply purchase the Switch 2 version at standard price.

**Do I need to buy the Rolling Live Highlights DLC to get mouse controls?**

No. Mouse controls, the smoother framerate, and the enhanced graphics are part of the free update that comes with the Switch 2 version. The Rolling Live Highlights DLC is separate add-on content covering new stages and a new soundtrack, so check the official listing for its pricing and availability.

**Is mouse control actually better than the analog stick?**

It depends on what you're doing. Player experience suggests mouse-style control is more precise for small corrections and tight grabs, while the stick remains excellent for broad, sweeping turns. Most players end up mixing both depending on the stage.

**Will my progress carry over if I upgrade?**

Progress is typically tied to your account and save data rather than the specific version you're playing, so upgrading on the same account and platform is the safest way to keep everything intact. Before you upgrade, make sure your save data is backed up or synced to the cloud if your platform supports it.
