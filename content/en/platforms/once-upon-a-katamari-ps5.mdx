The King of All Cosmos has wrecked the sky again, and only an extremely sticky ball can fix it. **Once Upon A Katamari PS5** sends the Prince rolling through the Jurassic Era, the Ice Age, and historic Japan to put the stars back where they belong. If you grew up chanting along to the series' joyful soundtrack while hoovering up pencils and pandas, this is the comeback you have been waiting for — and if you are brand new, **Once Upon A Katamari PS5** is a surprisingly friendly place to start.

This guide breaks down what the game actually is, how its time-travel structure works, what the multiplayer mode adds, and which edition is worth your money.

## A Katamari Story That Rolls Through Time

The premise is classic Katamari absurdity. The King, apparently on his best behavior, got hold of a peculiar scroll and accidentally crushed the Earth, the Moon, and a pile of planets. Your job is to roll across multiple historical eras and rebuild the dazzling field of stars.

That structure is the biggest shift from earlier entries. Instead of one continuous modern-day world, the game hops between periods, and each one changes how you approach a level.

| Era | What You Roll Through | How It Plays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jurassic Era | Dinosaurs, prehistoric terrain, ancient plants | Large, slow targets that create huge size jumps once you are big enough |
| Ice Age | Frozen landscapes, mammoths, ice formations | Slippery surfaces and tight paths reward careful steering |
| Historic Japan | Traditional towns, landmarks, everyday objects | Dense clusters of small items make it easy to build momentum fast |
| Present Day | Familiar modern objects and settings | A comfortable place to learn the core mechanics |
| Cosmic Stages | KatamariBall arenas | Built for the four-player competitive mode |

Because each era has its own object density, you cannot rely on one habit for the whole game. A route that works beautifully in a crowded historic town will feel empty and slow in the Ice Age. The best rollers learn to read a stage in the first thirty seconds and adjust.

## Playing as the Prince and His 68 Cousins

Katamari has always been a family affair, and this entry leans into it hard. You can play as the Prince, or pick from his 68 cousins and kin. Each one is selectable from the start of a run, so you are never locked into a single character.

The customization layer is where things get fun. You can adjust colors and facial features to build your own Cousin designs — and the game frames the possibilities as being as numerous as the stars in the sky. In practice, that means:

- **Color swapping** lets you match a Cousin to a stage's palette, which sounds cosmetic but makes it easier to spot your character in chaotic pile-ups.
- **Face customization** gives each design a personality, which matters more than you would expect when you are staring at the same character for hours.
- **Cousin variety** means you can rotate characters between attempts without losing progress or momentum.

For long-time fans, this is the feature that keeps the game alive well after the credits. Community reports from previous Katamari titles suggest that character customization is often the reason players return for "just one more run" — and this entry gives you far more to tinker with.

## KatamariBall: Four-Player Celestial Competition

The headline addition for multiplayer fans is KatamariBall, described in the game as the galactic sport of choice across the Cosmos. It supports up to four players, and you have two ways to play it.

| Mode | Players | Needs PS Plus? | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Story mode | 1 | No | Restoring the stars at your own pace |
| KatamariBall online | Up to 4 | Yes | Competing against cousins worldwide |
| KatamariBall offline | Up to 4 | No | Practicing against CPU opponents |

The offline option is genuinely useful. You can learn arena layouts, test how different sizes handle, and figure out when to chase opponents versus when to farm objects — all without touching a subscription. Then, when you go online, you are not the person who spends the first match spinning in circles.

A practical tip for KatamariBall newcomers: treat the first minute as a growth phase, not a fight. A player who is slightly smaller but positioned well usually beats a player who is bigger but stuck against a wall.

## Editions: Standard vs. Royal Sound Edition

There are two purchase paths, and the difference is almost entirely musical.

| Edition | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Standard Edition | Once Upon a Katamari (PS5) |
| Royal Sound Edition | The game plus Rolling Live Highlights, Dance Dance Remixes, Katamari Damacy Series Neo Remixes, Katamari Damacy Series Songs: Side A, and Katamari Damacy Series Songs: Side B |

The PlayStation Store listing also references a music-focused edition referred to as The King of All Sounds Edition, and it includes an explicit warning to be careful about duplicate purchases. That warning is worth taking seriously: if you already own one version of the soundtrack bundle, buying the upgraded edition again may hand you content you already have.

Katamari's music has always been half the appeal. Tracks like the series' signature vocal themes are the reason people hum along while rolling up entire city blocks. If you are the type of player who keeps a game's soundtrack on your phone, the Royal Sound Edition is the version aimed at you. If you just want to roll, the Standard Edition covers everything gameplay-related.

## Tips for Getting Bigger, Faster

Rolling a katamari looks simple until you are three minutes from the timer with a ball the size of a shopping cart. Here are habits that consistently pay off.

| Tip | Why It Works |
| --- | --- |
| Start with clusters, not singles | Rolling five small items in a row builds size faster than chasing one medium object |
| Respect the size gate | Objects that look rollable often are not until you cross a size threshold — test early, then commit |
| Revisit cleared zones | Areas you stripped clean early may have respawned or become newly rollable once you are bigger |
| Bank time, not perfection | Finishing a stage with a completed goal beats a perfect route that runs out of clock |
| Learn each era's rhythm | Dense eras reward speed; sparse eras reward route planning |

One more piece of advice that veteran Katamari players repeat constantly: do not fight the camera. Fighting the camera wastes the seconds you need for rolling. Adjust your movement, not the view.

## Is Once Upon A Katamari PS5 Worth It?

The answer depends on what you want from a Katamari game.

If you want a relaxed, weird, endlessly re-playable game that you can pick up for twenty minutes and put down without guilt, this delivers. The time-travel structure gives the campaign more variety than a single-setting entry, and the Cousin customization adds a creative layer that costs you nothing in gameplay terms.

If you want competitive multiplayer, KatamariBall gives you a reason to keep a PlayStation Plus subscription active — but remember that online play is optional, so you are never forced into it.

The game's rating sits comfortably in the casual-action space. It is not a punishing challenge; it is a toy box with a timer attached. That is exactly what the series has always been, and this entry understands its own identity.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need PlayStation Plus to play Once Upon A Katamari PS5?**
No. The single-player story and offline KatamariBall against CPU opponents work without a subscription. PlayStation Plus is only required if you want to play KatamariBall online against other players.

**How many players does KatamariBall support?**
Up to four players. You can compete online against other players around the world, or practice offline against CPU opponents.

**Can I play as characters other than the Prince?**
Yes. You can play as the Prince or choose among his 68 cousins and kin, and you can customize their colors and faces to create original designs.

**Which edition of Once Upon A Katamari PS5 should I buy?**
Buy the Standard Edition if you only care about gameplay. Choose the Royal Sound Edition if you want the additional remixes and soundtrack collections — just double-check that you are not repurchasing music you already own, since the store listing warns about duplicate purchases.

**What languages does the game support?**
Voice is in English, with screen languages available in English, French (France), and Spanish.
