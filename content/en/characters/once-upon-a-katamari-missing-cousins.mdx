## Why Once Upon A Katamari Missing Cousins Keep Slipping Away

Few things sting more than finishing a stage in Once Upon A Katamari with a beautiful roll and still coming up one cousin short. Hunting **Once Upon A Katamari missing cousins** is one of the most common frustrations in the game's collectible loop, and here's the reassuring part: it's almost never a bug. In the vast majority of cases, **Once Upon A Katamari missing cousins** are simply waiting behind a rule you haven't triggered yet — a spawn order, a stage unlock, or a time-travel trip you haven't made.

Cousins in this entry don't all sit in the open, waiting politely to be rolled up. Many are gated behind order-of-collection logic, and the game offers very little feedback when you've tripped one of those gates. You'll sweep a stage clean, see an empty spot where a guide promised a cousin, and assume the collectible is broken. Usually it isn't.

Before you blame the game, run through the three most common causes.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Quick fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cousin counter frozen after a full clear | The currently active cousin on that stage was never rolled up | Re-enter the stage and grab the one that actually spawned |
| A guide lists a cousin here, but the spot is empty | An earlier cousin on the same stage is still uncollected | Collect that stage's cousins in order, one per clear |
| A cousin appears once, then vanishes mid-run | Katamari size threshold — community reports cite roughly 60m on certain stages | Grab it early, before you outgrow the window |
| Cousin rolled up, but the roster didn't change | Stage wasn't completed after the pickup (player experience) | Finish the level instead of quitting out |

The pattern in that table matters more than any single row. Almost every "missing" cousin report traces back to one of these four situations, and all four are fixable in a single extra run.

## The Golden Rule: Cousins Spawn One at a Time

This is the single most important thing to internalize about Once Upon A Katamari collectibles: **on stages with multiple cousins, only one cousin is available at a time.** The next one will not spawn until you have cleared the stage with the previous cousin rolled up.

That rule comes straight from community documentation — both the PowerPyx collectible guide and the Steam community guide for finding all 69 cousins state it explicitly. It also explains why so many players hit a wall: they visit a stage, grab the one cousin that's visible, leave, and then wonder why the second and third cousins on their checklist never show up.

Here's how the sequence typically plays out on a multi-cousin stage.

| Attempt | Cousin that can appear | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 1st clear | Cousin #1 | Available from the start |
| 2nd clear | Cousin #2 | Previous run completed with Cousin #1 rolled up |
| 3rd clear | Cousin #3 | Previous run completed with Cousin #2 rolled up |
| Further clears | Nothing new | That stage's set is exhausted |

Two details trip people up constantly. First, the pickup has to be followed by an actual stage completion — rolling a cousin up and then quitting to the menu is a widely reported way to "lose" progress. Second, the cousins themselves don't move between attempts; the *spawn slot* changes. If you memorized where Cousin #1 sat, that same spot may be empty on your next run while a new cousin waits somewhere else entirely on the map.

That second point is why so many players describe Once Upon A Katamari missing cousins as "invisible." They're not invisible — they're just in a different hiding spot than the last one.

## The 69 vs. 39 Cousin Mystery, Explained

If you've browsed community forums, you've seen the confusion: the launch trailer and multiple guides promise 69 cousins, yet some in-game counters and unlock requirements reference a much smaller number, with 39 coming up repeatedly in player discussions.

The short answer, based on community reports, is that the roster is split into groups rather than presented as one flat total. A stage or gate that asks for 39 cousins is checking a subset, not the entire collection. Meanwhile, guides written around the "find all 69" framing are counting every cousin hidden across every stage and era.

| Number you'll see | What it most likely represents | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| 69 | Total cousins hidden across the game's stages, per guides | Community walkthroughs and Steam guides |
| 39 | A subset used by an unlock requirement | In-game progression gates |
| 11 | Brand-new cousins added by this entry | Wiki documentation on the new roster |
| 117 | All cousins across the mainline series, including spin-offs | Wiki tallies across the franchise |

The takeaway: don't panic when a counter seems to contradict a guide. Different screens are counting different things, and none of them are lying to you.

## A Step-by-Step Plan for Filling the Roster

Rather than wandering from stage to stage hoping something new appears, work through a fixed routine. This is the process experienced players describe when they explain how they finished their collections.

1. **Clear the stage normally first.** Learn the layout before you hunt. You can't spot a cousin you don't recognize.
2. **Roll up whichever cousin is currently active.** If you see one, it's the one the game wants you to take.
3. **Finish the level.** Progress registers on completion. Do not quit out.
4. **Re-enter and sweep the whole map again.** The next cousin spawns in a new location, so old memorized routes won't help.
5. **Repeat until the stage stops producing.** When a stage gives you nothing new after a full clear, that set is finished.
6. **Check other eras with the Prince Ship.** New cousins tied to time travel won't appear otherwise.
7. **Cross-reference a checklist.** A written guide is the only reliable way to confirm what's genuinely absent versus what's just waiting its turn.

## Where Cousins Hide Most Often

Community walkthroughs consistently flag the same kinds of spots as the worst offenders for Once Upon A Katamari missing cousins. Cousins are rarely placed in the middle of an open plaza where you'd trip over them — they're tucked into geometry you roll past at speed.

- **Behind and under water features.** Logs spanning rivers and ponds are a recurring hiding spot, with cousins tucked on the far side or beneath the crossing.
- **Second areas of large stages.** Players repeatedly note that a cousin sits in a stage's later zone, past the point where most runs naturally end.
- **Along walls and under overhangs.** Anything that breaks line of sight from your default rolling path.
- **Near size-gated terrain.** If a cousin sits somewhere you can only reach after growing, you may be arriving too late or too early.

A useful habit: on any stage where you suspect a cousin is hiding, do one dedicated sweep with a small katamari. Small katamaris move through tight spaces that a large one simply can't enter, and you'll see details you'd otherwise plow straight through.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why do I still have Once Upon A Katamari missing cousins after clearing every stage?**
In most cases, you've collected a stage's first cousin but never returned to trigger the second. Cousins on the same stage spawn one at a time, and the next only appears after a completed run with the previous one rolled up. Check your multi-cousin stages first.

**Does the game have 69 cousins or 39?**
Community reports suggest the roster is split into groups. The 69 figure reflects the total hidden across stages and guides, while 39 shows up in specific in-game unlock requirements. They're counting different sets, not contradicting each other.

**Do I need to finish a stage after picking up a cousin?**
Player experience strongly suggests yes. Quitting out after a pickup is one of the most commonly reported ways to end up with a cousin that never registered.

**How many new cousins does Once Upon A Katamari add?**
Wiki documentation credits this entry with 11 new cousins, reached through the Prince Ship's time travel mechanic. Their official titles weren't documented at the time that reference was compiled, so expect the community to fill in those details over time.
