Most stages in this series ask you to roll until the clock runs out. The **Once Upon A Katamari race** stage "As Large As Possible" flips that expectation: it hands you a seven-minute timer and one hard target — grow to 8 meters before time expires. If you have ever stalled out two sizes short with thirty seconds left, you already know why this stage deserves its own guide.

Treating the Once Upon A Katamari race like a sightseeing tour is the fastest way to lose it. Treating it like a route-planning problem is the fastest way to win. This guide breaks down the stage rules, the timing windows that matter, and the crown tucked away on a hilltop.

## What Is the "As Large As Possible (Race)" Stage?

"Once Upon A Katamari" is the seventh entry in the Katamari series, and it structures its content around 10 stages known as Eras — nine of which drop the Prince into a distinct time period. "As Large As Possible (Race)" sits among them as the game's clearest test of pure growth speed.

Despite the word "race" in the title, you are not chasing a rival around a track. Community walkthroughs confirm the sole objective is size: hit 8 meters within 7 minutes. The pressure comes from object density and your own routing, not from an opponent.

| Detail | Value |
|---|---|
| Stage name | As Large As Possible (Race) |
| Primary objective | Reach 8 m in size |
| Time limit | 7 minutes |
| Real opponent | The clock (no rival racer) |
| Signature collectible | A crown on a hilltop |
| Core skill tested | Route planning and size gating |

That last row is the one most players underestimate. Katamari rules mean you can only roll up objects smaller than your current diameter, so a bad first minute locks you out of the objects you need in minute five.

## How the Once Upon A Katamari Race Mode Actually Works

Every Katamari stage is secretly a gate-checking puzzle. Your katamari can only absorb objects below its current size, which creates a soft progression ladder: floor clutter, then furniture, then street props, then vehicles, then architecture.

In a race stage, that ladder is compressed. You have a fixed timer and a fixed finish line, so any minute spent rolling in a zone you have already outgrown is a minute permanently lost. According to the [official Once Upon A KATAMARI page from Bandai Namco](https://www.bandainamcoent.com/games/once-upon-a-katamari), the game leans into new stage designs and a fresh soundtrack — and the race stage is where that design philosophy feels most like a puzzle box.

Here is how the Once Upon A Katamari race differs from a typical size stage:

| Feature | Race stage | Typical size stage |
|---|---|---|
| Win condition | Hit 8 m before the timer hits zero | Hit a target size, often with more slack |
| Timer pressure | High — 7 minutes, no extensions | Moderate |
| Optimal playstyle | Fast, linear routing | Exploration and collection |
| Failure state | Missing the size target | Missing the target or an objective |
| Replay value | Route optimization and score chasing | Collectible cleanup |

The practical takeaway: in a normal stage you can wander. In a race, wandering is the failure state.

## Step-by-Step: A Winning Route for the Once Upon A Katamari Race

Community reports consistently point to the same structural approach: treat the seven minutes as four distinct phases, and never revisit a zone once it stops feeding you.

| Phase | Suggested split | What to roll | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Clutter rush | First ~60 seconds | Small indoor and ground-level items | Builds the size floor that unlocks everything else |
| 2. Street sweep | Next ~90 seconds | Street furniture, signs, small vehicles | Fastest growth-per-second window in the stage |
| 3. Vehicle and structure push | Middle ~2 minutes | Cars, larger props, low buildings | Where most runs either pull ahead or fall behind |
| 4. Finisher | Final ~90 seconds | Landmarks and anything large left nearby | Closes the gap to 8 m if you are short |

A few habits make those phases work in practice:

- **Move in one direction.** Looping back through cleared streets wastes seconds and gives you nothing.
- **Roll in a straight-ish line while small.** Tight turns cost momentum when your katamari is tiny and the clock is loud.
- **Reassess at the halfway mark.** If you are behind pace, switch to the densest object cluster you can currently absorb rather than hunting for the perfect item.
- **Save the big stuff for last.** Large objects take longer to approach; grabbing them too early wastes time you need for volume.

Player experience suggests the biggest single improvement most people can make is simply not stopping. A mediocre route rolled continuously beats a perfect route rolled in bursts.

## Common Mistakes That Cost You the Once Upon A Katamari Race

Failing this stage almost never comes down to reflexes. It comes down to three or four repeatable decisions.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Rolling into objects that are too large | You bounce off and lose momentum | Check your size before committing to a cluster |
| Sticking to one small area | Object density depletes fast | Rotate to a fresh zone every 30–45 seconds |
| Ignoring the timer until the end | No time left to correct a slow start | Check the clock at each phase transition |
| Chasing the crown mid-run | It sits on a hilltop, off the fastest route | Grab it on a dedicated replay |
| Panicking below 8 m | Erratic movement kills growth rate | Target the largest absorbable objects immediately |

That crown row deserves emphasis. "As Large As Possible (Race)" contains a crown positioned on top of a hill. Collectible guides describe it as large and easy to spot, but easy to spot is not the same as easy to reach on a seven-minute clock.

If you are chasing full completion, split your goals: one run for the clear, a separate run for the crown. Trying to do both at once is how a comfortable 8 m becomes a frustrating 7.4 m.

## Crown Hunting and Collectibles in the Once Upon A Katamari Race

The game packs a substantial collectible layer on top of its Era structure — 248 collectibles across crowns, cousins, and presents, according to community tracking guides. Race stages contribute to that total, and they are usually the ones players finish last because the timer discourages detours.

Here is a practical split for collectible hunting in this stage:

| Goal | Route approach | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Just clear the stage | Fastest linear path, ignore hilltops | Prioritize density over landmarks |
| Grab the crown | Detour to the hilltop once you are large enough | Reported as large and visually obvious |
| Full collectible sweep | Clear first, then hunt with a comfortable size | Reduces timer anxiety and missed pickups |

Once your katamari is comfortably past the mid-range, the hilltop crown becomes a much safer detour. Going for it while still small usually means a slow climb and a lost run.

## Why This Stage Matters More Than It Looks

"As Large As Possible (Race)" functions as a skill check for everything the rest of the game asks of you. It teaches size gating, route discipline, and the value of forward momentum — the three fundamentals that carry into every other Era.

It is also one of the most replayable stages in "Once Upon A Katamari." Because the objective is a threshold rather than a specific collection, there is always a faster route, a cleaner line, or a bigger finish. Community reports frequently describe players shaving seconds off their clears long after earning the crown, purely for the satisfaction of a tighter run.

If you are new to the game, clear it once with a relaxed route. If you are experienced, treat the Once Upon A Katamari race as a routing puzzle and see how much size you can bank before the timer runs dry.

## FAQ

**How long is the Once Upon A Katamari race stage?**
It runs for 7 minutes, and the win condition is reaching 8 meters in size before the timer expires. There is no extension or overtime.

**Do I race against another character in "As Large As Possible (Race)"?**
No. Despite the name, the stage is a solo size challenge. Your opponent is the clock and the object layout, which is why routing matters more than speed alone.

**Where is the crown in the Once Upon A Katamari race stage?**
Community collectible guides place it on top of a hill. It is described as large and easy to spot, but reaching it costs time, so most players grab it on a separate run.

**What is the fastest way to fail this stage?**
Rolling into objects that are too large for your current size. Each bounce costs momentum, and in a seven-minute window, lost momentum is the same as lost time.

**Should I hunt collectibles during my first clear?**
No. Clear the stage first, then return with a bigger katamari and a relaxed timer to sweep up the crown and anything else you missed.
