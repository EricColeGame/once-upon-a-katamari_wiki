## What Once Upon A Katamari Endless Mode Actually Is

Every Katamari stage ends the same way: just as the really good stuff comes into reach, the timer runs out. Once Upon A Katamari endless mode exists to fix that specific heartbreak. Instead of racing a countdown, you keep rolling, growing, and exploring until you decide you are finished — no fail state, no target size, no pressure. For longtime fans, this is the mode that turns a five-minute sprint into a genuine sandbox, and it is usually the first thing players chase after clearing a stage. This guide covers what Once Upon A Katamari endless mode is, how players unlock it, and the strategies that separate a decent run from a record-setting one.

The series has used the name **Eternal Mode** for this concept for two decades, and community reports suggest the newest entry follows that tradition closely. The King of All Cosmos stops yelling about deadlines, the stage's music keeps looping, and the katamari you are pushing becomes a toy instead of a test.

A few things typically carry over into an Eternal run:

- **The full object layout.** Nothing is removed or re-skinned, so the stage you learned in Story Mode is the stage you get.
- **Stage gimmicks.** Ramps, water, narrow alleys, and anything else that shaped your route still applies.
- **Collectibles.** Presents, cousins, and hidden items remain grabbable — arguably more so, since you are not sprinting past them.
- **Your personal records.** Most entries track your biggest katamari per stage, which gives endless runs a scoreboard even without a clock.

What does *not* carry over is the pressure. Depending on the stage, you may start from the ground up and rebuild your katamari, which is part of the appeal: you get the whole growth curve, just without the stopwatch. For official details on the game itself, see [Bandai Namco Entertainment's official site](https://www.bandainamcoent.com/).

## How Endless Mode Compares to Story Mode and Time Attack

It helps to think of the modes as three different questions. Story Mode asks "can you grow fast enough?" Time Attack asks "can you grow faster than everyone else?" Endless mode asks "how far do you want to go?"

| Feature | Story Mode | Time Attack | Endless (Eternal) Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time limit | Yes | Yes, often tighter | None |
| Fail state | Yes | Yes | No |
| Target size | Yes | Yes | None |
| Object layout | Full | Full | Full |
| Pressure level | Moderate | High | None |
| Best for | Progression and unlocks | Score chasing | Collection, exploration, records |
| Collectibles | Yes | Yes, but risky | Yes, at your own pace |
| Typical session length | 3–6 minutes | 1–3 minutes | As long as you like |

The practical difference shows up in how you route a stage. In Time Attack you take the shortest path to the biggest objects you can reach. In endless mode you can detour down a side street just to see what is there — and that detour is often how players find cousins and presents they never noticed during the campaign.

## How to Unlock Once Upon A Katamari Endless Mode

Unlock conditions vary by entry, and community reports indicate that Once Upon A Katamari ties Eternal Mode to clearing the corresponding Story Mode stage. That is the same pattern the series has used for years, so it is a safe place to start.

| Step | What to do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Play the stage in Story Mode | Endless mode is usually a reward for clearing, not a menu option from the start |
| 2 | Reach the target size before time expires | Hitting the goal is the standard gate |
| 3 | Return to the stage select screen | A new mode toggle or icon typically appears next to the stage |
| 4 | Select the Eternal / Endless option | This loads the same stage with the timer disabled |
| 5 | Roll with no clock | Your run ends only when you choose to stop |

A couple of practical notes from player experience. First, if a stage does not show an endless option after a clear, re-check whether you actually hit the required size rather than simply surviving to the buzzer — some stages are stricter than others. Second, unlock progress is usually tracked per stage, so you will need to clear each level you want to explore freely. There is no single global switch that opens the whole game.

## Stage Picks and Rolling Strategy for Endless Runs

Not every stage is equally fun without a timer. Some are built for speed and feel empty once the urgency is gone; others open up beautifully. Here is how the common stage archetypes tend to play out.

| Stage type | Why it works in endless mode | Growth ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| Neighborhood / suburban | Dense mix of small and mid-size objects, easy early ramp | Medium to high |
| City center | Skyscrapers, vehicles, and landmarks all reachable | Very high |
| Indoor / house | Tight spaces force careful routing | Low |
| Waterfront / harbor | Boats and cranes create big jumps in size | High |
| Space / cosmic | Endgame scaling with planets and stars | Maximum |

### The early stretch (roughly 0 cm to 30 cm)

This is the part that feels most different from Story Mode, because there is no reason to panic. Sweep rooms methodically, take every small object in a tight loop, and avoid wandering into open areas where nothing is rollable yet.

### The middle stretch (about 1 m to 30 m)

Once you can pick up people, furniture, and small vehicles, the map effectively doubles. This is the best window for collectible hunting, because you are big enough to reach odd corners but still small enough to fit through alleys and doorways.

### The endgame (100 m and beyond)

At this point the stage is basically a buffet. The only real risk is boredom-driven sloppiness — clipping a wall and losing momentum. Long, looping routes that sweep the densest parts of the map beat frantic zig-zagging.

| Size milestone | What to target | Common trap |
|---|---|---|
| 0–30 cm | Small clutter, food, toys | Wasting time in empty plazas |
| 30 cm–1 m | Pets, plants, small appliances | Ignoring interior rooms |
| 1–10 m | People, benches, scooters | Getting stuck in narrow streets |
| 10–100 m | Cars, houses, trees | Rolling in circles instead of expanding outward |
| 100 m+ | Buildings, landmarks | Losing the route and doubling back |

### Five mistakes that stall an endless run

1. **Treating it like Time Attack.** Speed is no longer the goal; coverage is.
2. **Sticking to one neighborhood.** The biggest objects are usually at the map's edges.
3. **Ignoring vertical space.** Rooftops and upper floors often hide large, easy targets.
4. **Skipping collectibles.** Without a clock, there is no excuse to miss a present.
5. **Quitting early.** Endless mode rewards patience; the last ten minutes are usually the most productive.

## Collection Goals, Cousins, and Long-Term Play

The real long-term value of Once Upon A Katamari endless mode is that it turns each stage into a checklist you can actually finish. Story Mode gives you one shot at a collectible before the buzzer; an Eternal run gives you as many attempts as you want.

| Goal | What to look for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cousins | Hidden characters tucked near landmarks | Often placed where a timed run would never send you |
| Presents | Wrapped gift boxes | Unlock accessories for the Prince |
| Royal / special items | Rare one-off objects | Frequently placed at the map's far edges |
| Biggest katamari record | Your personal best per stage | The main scoreboard in a mode without a clock |
| Full object log | Every rollable item in the stage | The slowest but most satisfying completion goal |

Player experience suggests that collectible hunting is dramatically easier once you have grown past roughly the 10 m mark, because you can reach areas that were physically blocked earlier in the stage. The tradeoff is that you also become large enough to accidentally roll over the very thing you were trying to grab — so slow down when you spot something small.

A practical routine that works well: do one fast "growth lap" to build size, then one slow "collection lap" at that size, then a final growth push. Three passes, no timer, no stress.

## FAQ

**Is Once Upon A Katamari endless mode the same thing as Eternal Mode?**
Functionally, yes. "Eternal Mode" is the series' traditional name for a stage with the time limit removed, and community reports use the two terms interchangeably when discussing Once Upon A Katamari endless mode.

**Do I need to beat the game to unlock endless mode?**
Based on the series' history and community reports, unlocks are typically tied to clearing individual stages rather than finishing the entire campaign. Clear a stage, and that stage usually becomes available to roll freely.

**Does anything carry over between endless runs?**
Your collectibles and records generally persist, but your katamari size resets when you restart. That is why the mode is best treated as a series of separate expeditions rather than one continuous save.

**Is there any downside to playing endless mode?**
Only opportunity cost. Because there is no timer, it is easy to spend an hour on a stage that gives no progression rewards. Use it for collection goals and personal records, and go back to Story Mode when you want to advance.

**Can I still fail in endless mode?**
In the traditional version of the mode, no — there is no time limit and no target size, so the only way a run ends is when you decide it does. That freedom is exactly what makes Once Upon A Katamari endless mode the most relaxing way to play.
