Once Upon A Katamari multiplayer is the headline attraction of the first new Katamari Damacy entry in 14 years — a four-player cosmic sport called KatamariBall that pits your rolling instincts against cousins from around the world.

For a series long defined by solo, meditative rolling, the arrival of Once Upon A Katamari multiplayer changes how you approach every stage: the objects you grab and the routes you take become contests, not just puzzles.

This guide breaks down what the official materials confirm about the mode, how online and CPU play differ, and the habits that separate a clean victory from a tangled mess of a match.

## KatamariBall Explained: What Once Upon A Katamari Multiplayer Actually Is

The setup is classic Katamari chaos. The King of All Cosmos was fiddling with a peculiar scroll when he accidentally destroyed the Earth and countless stars, so the Prince has to roll across time itself — through the Jurassic Period, the Ice Age, historic Japan, and other eras — to rebuild the glittering night sky.

KatamariBall is where that rolling turns competitive. Bandai Namco describes it as the galactic sport of choice across the Cosmos, played by up to four competitors at once, either against online cousins worldwide or against CPU opponents offline.

| Detail | What's confirmed | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| Mode name | KatamariBall | A dedicated competitive mode, not a story variant |
| Player count | Up to four players ("Celestial 4-Player KatamariBall") | Small lobbies keep every match fast and personal |
| Opponents | Online cousins or offline CPU opponents | You can practice without waiting in a lobby |
| Playable characters | The Prince, 68 cousins and kin, plus custom designs | Cosmetic freedom never blocks you from competing |
| Signature tool | The magnet, which draws in nearby objects | Item control can matter more than raw speed |
| Bonus content | Royal Sound Edition adds 6 cousins and 18 costume items | More ways to make your character stand out |

The important takeaway is scale. Four-player KatamariBall is not a battle royale where you can hide in a corner. With so few competitors, every route you take is visible, and every cluster of objects you leave behind becomes someone else's gain.

### How it differs from solo rolling

Solo play in Once Upon A Katamari is a puzzle action adventure built around size targets, timers, and the King's theatrical disappointment. KatamariBall replaces the timer as your main opponent with a human being who wants exactly the same object pile you do.

In player experience terms, that shift is dramatic. A route that feels efficient against a clock can feel reckless against three rivals who are all watching the same crowded intersection.

## Online vs. Offline: Choosing Your Once Upon A Katamari Multiplayer Setup

One of the smartest design decisions in Once Upon A Katamari multiplayer is that it never forces you online. You can compete against cousins around the world, or you can sharpen your skills against CPU opponents in offline matches.

That flexibility matters more than it sounds. Rolling a katamari is a game of feel — the weight of the ball, the timing of a turn, the moment a cluster is dense enough to be worth a detour. Learning that feel under online pressure is a quick way to develop bad habits.

| Factor | Online matches | Offline CPU matches |
|---|---|---|
| Opponent behavior | Unpredictable, improvised | Consistent, learnable patterns |
| Connection quality | Affects the experience | Never an issue |
| Best for | Testing real skill and bragging rights | Learning controls and testing routes |
| Pressure level | High | Low to moderate |
| Session length | Short, punchy bursts | As long as you like |

A simple routine works well: warm up offline for a few matches, then take that muscle memory online. Player experience with matchmaking will vary by region and time of day, so patience during quieter hours helps.

### Do you need to finish the story first?

Official materials do not describe a story gate blocking KatamariBall. Even so, playing through the opening stages of the campaign is the fastest way to internalize turning, dashing, and object priority before you face real opponents.

## Practical Tips for Winning More KatamariBall Matches

KatamariBall rewards planning more than reflexes. These habits consistently separate players who grow fast from players who spend the match chasing one oversized object.

- **Warm up against CPU opponents first.** Two or three offline matches prime your timing without costing you a loss.
- **Learn your opening route.** The first fifteen seconds set your size trajectory; a clean start beats a clever comeback.
- **Chase density, not size.** Five small objects in one sweep usually beat one large object across the map.
- **Use the magnet deliberately.** It draws in nearby objects, so trigger it in the middle of a cluster rather than at the edge of one.
- **Plan a comeback path.** Know one dense route you can run when you fall behind instead of improvising in a panic.
- **Keep your character visible.** Distinct colors help you read your position when the screen gets crowded.

| Common mistake | Why it hurts | Better approach |
|---|---|---|
| Sprinting toward the biggest object on screen | Long detours and slow early growth | Sweep dense clusters first |
| Firing the magnet in empty space | Wastes the tool's pull | Activate it inside a cluster |
| Rolling in predictable straight lines | Easy for rivals to cut you off | Loop and change direction often |
| Panicking when trailing | Sloppy turns and missed pickups | Reset and run your comeback route |
| Skipping CPU practice entirely | Slower map knowledge | Warm up offline before going online |

### Practice like it's a sport

KatamariBall is described as a galactic sport, and it plays like one. Short, repeated sessions beat marathon grinds because each match is a chance to test one specific improvement — a tighter turn, a faster opening, a smarter magnet timing.

## FAQ: Once Upon A Katamari Multiplayer

**Is Once Upon A Katamari multiplayer online only?**
No. KatamariBall supports both online matches against cousins around the world and offline matches against CPU opponents, so you can play competitively without an internet connection.

**How many players can join a KatamariBall match?**
The mode is billed as a celestial four-player experience, meaning up to four competitors roll at once.

**Can I use my own custom character in Once Upon A Katamari multiplayer?**
Yes. The customization system lets you build original characters with different colors and faces, and those designs are meant to stand out in KatamariBall.

**Do I need to beat the story before playing KatamariBall?**
Official materials don't describe a story requirement. Playing the early campaign stages is still the best way to learn rolling fundamentals before facing human opponents.

**Is there cross-platform play?**
Bandai Namco's official materials don't detail platform-specific matchmaking features, so check the official site or store page for your platform for the most current information.
