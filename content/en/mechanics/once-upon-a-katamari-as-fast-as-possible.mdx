## What the As Fast As Possible Stages Actually Ask of You

The As Fast As Possible family is the game's purest efficiency test. Rather than handing you a wide-open sandbox and a generous timer, each level gives you a target size and asks you to reach it quickly. Every Once Upon A Katamari as fast as possible level follows the same underlying rule: **growth is the clock.**

The stages scale up aggressively, and knowing the shape of each one before you start is half the battle.

| Stage | Goal | What it really tests |
|---|---|---|
| As Fast As Possible 1 | Reach 15 cm | Basic rolling, tiny-object pickup priority |
| As Fast As Possible 3 | Reach 3 m within the time limit | Threshold chaining without dead ends |
| As Fast As Possible 5 | Extreme scale (community reports a ~1000 m objective) | Long-run stamina and route planning |
| As Fast As Possible (Ghost Ship) | Listed as its own speedrun category | Route memorization and repeatability |

The first stage is essentially a movement exam. Fifteen centimeters is a tiny target, so you win or lose on how cleanly you scoop up small objects in a dense starting area rather than on any grand strategy. Stage 3 is the sister stage to As Large As Possible 3, and it's where threshold management starts to matter — you need to hit 3 m inside the limit, which means never wandering into a zone full of objects you still can't pick up.

Later entries balloon the scale. According to player experience shared in community discussions, there's a turning point around 300 meters where the world opens up, after which the push toward roughly 1000 meters goes quickly. One community member reported finishing that stage in under nine minutes using that approach.

## The Four Mechanics That Decide Your Clear Time

You can't out-practice a bad route, but you also can't route your way around sloppy rolling. These four mechanics are where nearly all your time is won or lost.

| Mechanic | How to use it | Impact on your clear |
|---|---|---|
| Rolling lines | Pick a lane and commit; avoid clipping large objects | High — momentum loss compounds |
| Alternating-stick pump | Rapidly rotate both sticks to build speed | High — your main speed tool |
| Both-stick pivot | Press both sticks down and rotate to turn sharply | Medium — saves wide, wasted arcs |
| Size thresholds | Re-read the field after every growth milestone | Very high — unlocks your next tier |

### Rolling lines beat rolling everything

New players instinctively steer toward the nearest object. Faster players steer toward the nearest *dense cluster* of objects they can actually pick up. A straight 4-second run through a crowded alley beats a 6-second zigzag that grabs slightly more mass but kills your speed twice.

### The both-stick pivot is your turning brake

Wide turns are silent time sinks. The series-standard both-stick pivot lets you reorient almost in place, which matters enormously in tight starting areas where the 15 cm goal is decided in the first few seconds.

### Rockets are a timing decision, not a pickup

The Rocket item makes your katamari roll faster for a limited time. The mistake is grabbing it and immediately burning it while you're still turning through clutter. Instead:

- Grab the Rocket only when a long, straight, dense stretch is directly ahead.
- Never activate it while pivoting or reversing.
- Save it for the moment right after you cross a size threshold, when a whole new tier of objects opens up.

### Thresholds are your real checkpoints

Every size milestone changes which objects are legal pickups. Fast runs treat each threshold as a reset point: stop steering toward the old tier, and immediately re-aim at the newly available tier.

## Routing Each Stage: A Practical Order of Operations

Routing Once Upon A Katamari as fast as possible comes down to a repeatable loop. Use this sequence on any stage in the family.

1. **Scout the starting zone before moving.** Identify the two densest clusters of pickup-legal objects.
2. **Open with a straight line through cluster one.** Do not chase stragglers.
3. **Pivot, then take cluster two.** Keep the pivot under a second.
4. **Check your size.** The moment you cross a threshold, abandon your old route entirely.
5. **Escalate outward.** Each new tier should pull you toward bigger, denser object fields.
6. **Spend your Rocket on the longest open stretch you've found.**

| Situation | Fast response | Slow response |
|---|---|---|
| Just crossed a threshold | Turn toward newly legal objects | Keep farming the old tier |
| Surrounded by too-large objects | Pivot out and re-enter a smaller field | Push into them and stall |
| Rocket available mid-route | Bank it for the next open lane | Pop it immediately |
| Time pressure near the end | Commit to one dense cluster | Wander looking for a "better" one |

## Common Mistakes That Cost You Seconds

Most failed attempts aren't dramatic. They're a dozen small inefficiencies stacked on top of each other.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Grabbing one object at a time | Constant speed resets | Aim for clusters, not objects |
| Wasting the Rocket on a turn | Limited duration expires unused | Bank it for a straight lane |
| Over-rolling past a threshold | You keep farming an obsolete tier | Re-route the instant you grow |
| Wide, drifting turns | Distance with no mass gained | Use the both-stick pivot |
| Restarting too fast after a bad start | You never learn the route | Finish the attempt, then adjust |

The last one deserves emphasis. Players chasing Once Upon A Katamari as fast as possible clears often reset after five seconds of imperfect play and never discover that the second half of the stage was the actual problem. Finish your runs. Diagnose them. Then reset.

## A Simple Practice Routine

You don't need a stopwatch to get faster — you need repetition with a purpose. This routine fits into a single sitting.

| Drill | What to do | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Line drill | Roll 30 seconds using only straight lines and pivots | Eliminate drifting |
| Threshold drill | Play As Fast As Possible 1 and re-route after every pickup tier | Build threshold awareness |
| Rocket drill | Bank the Rocket until a long open lane appears | Learn item patience |
| Full-attempt drill | Complete As Fast As Possible 3 without resetting | Build route memory |

Run the drills in order. The line drill fixes your baseline movement, the threshold drill teaches you to read the field, and the full-attempt drill converts both into a repeatable route. Most players see their stage times drop noticeably within a handful of sessions.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to clear Once Upon A Katamari as fast as possible stages?**

Early stages like As Fast As Possible 1 are measured in seconds of decision-making rather than minutes — the 15 cm target is small, so a clean opening usually decides it. Later entries stretch out considerably, and community reports put the hardest stage at under nine minutes for an experienced player.

**What's the single biggest time-saver?**

Threshold awareness. The instant you grow into a new pickup tier, your old route becomes inefficient. Re-aiming immediately is worth more than any individual trick.

**Should I use the Rocket as soon as I find it?**

No. The Rocket only makes your katamari roll faster for a limited time, so activate it when you're pointed down a long, dense, straight lane. Popping it mid-turn wastes most of its duration.

**Do I need to collect Crowns to clear these stages quickly?**

No. Crowns are collectibles, not objectives. They're worth grabbing on a dedicated collectible run, but during a speed attempt they're a detour — skip them unless one happens to sit directly on your route.
