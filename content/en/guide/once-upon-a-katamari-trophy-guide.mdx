The Prince is back, and the urge to roll everything in sight into one enormous ball has come with him. If you're chasing the platinum, a solid Once Upon A Katamari trophy guide saves you from replaying the same stage five times for a single missed item. Katamari trophy lists have a reputation for being cozy and low-stress, and that's mostly deserved — until you hit the collection milestones. Those are the ones that quietly turn a relaxing roll-em-up into a checklist nightmare. The good news is that almost everything in this game can be planned around, as long as you know the order to tackle things in.

## Why Planning Beats Brute Force in This Trophy List

The Katamari formula rewards improvisation, but trophy hunting rewards consistency. Rolling the same stage repeatedly with no plan burns hours, especially when a single trophy requires a specific object category you keep rolling past.

Player experience from earlier entries in the series points to one universal rule: **your first playthrough should be a discovery run, not a completion run.** Learn the stage layouts, note where clusters of similar objects sit, and resist the urge to restart when a run goes sideways. Restarting mid-stage rarely helps, because most trophies track cumulative progress across attempts anyway.

The other big reason to plan: many trophy conditions overlap. A single well-planned run can knock out a size target, a collection milestone, and a stage-clear trophy at the same time. A little routing knowledge turns three runs into one.

### What Makes This List Different From Older Katamari Games

Modern Katamari titles tend to be more generous with checkpoints and stage select, which softens the difficulty curve considerably. Community reports suggest that as long as you keep a mental (or written) checklist of item categories, nothing here should hard-wall you. The challenge is memory and organization, not reflexes.

## Trophy Categories: What You're Actually Signing Up For

Trophy lists across the series follow a recognizable pattern. Here's the breakdown of the categories you can expect to see, and how much attention each one demands.

| Trophy Category | What It Typically Asks | Missable? | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Story progression | Clear every stage in the main campaign | No | Low |
| Size milestones | Hit a target diameter within the time limit | Sometimes | Medium |
| Collection milestones | Roll up specific counts of a certain item type | Yes | Medium–High |
| Character & cousin unlocks | Find hidden characters scattered across stages | Yes | Medium |
| Performance challenges | Fast clears, high scores, no-fail runs | Yes | High |
| Miscellaneous actions | Odd one-off stunts and easter eggs | Yes | Low–Medium |

If you're short on time, the smart move is to knock out the progression and size trophies first, since those come naturally. Save the collection and performance tiers for a dedicated cleanup phase.

### The Collection Trophies Are the Real Boss Fight

Nothing in a Katamari game is genuinely hard in the traditional sense — but finding the *last* of something is. Whether it's a specific object class, a rare item tucked in a corner, or a hidden character, the final few entries on any collection checklist will eat more time than the entire story mode.

Keep a running note as you play. If you spot an unusual object — something that doesn't match the visual theme of the stage — roll it up immediately. That instinct saves you a full replay later.

## The Phase-by-Phase Platinum Roadmap

This is the core of any good Once Upon A Katamari trophy guide: a sequence that respects how the game unlocks content. Follow the phases in order and you'll avoid the classic trap of chasing a trophy you can't earn yet.

| Phase | Focus | Why It Comes Here | Watch Out For |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Discovery run | Play every stage, learn layouts | Unlocks stages, characters, and mechanics | Don't restart for score |
| 2. Size targets | Replay for diameter goals | Easier once you know the map | Timer pressure |
| 3. Character hunting | Find hidden cousins and characters | Requires stage familiarity | Missable spawns |
| 4. Collection cleanup | Chase item-count trophies | Cumulative, benefits from planning | Duplicate counting rules |
| 5. Performance push | Fast clears and high scores | Needs full mastery of controls | Frustration resets |
| 6. Mop-up | Any stragglers and misc actions | Last by definition | Forgetting to save |

Working through the list in this order means every run after Phase 1 is productive. Doing it out of order is how people end up with a hundred-hour platinum.

### How Long Does It Take?

Time estimates for any trophy list vary wildly by player, so treat any specific number you see online as a rough guide at best. What matters is *relative* effort: the story and size trophies come quickly, the character and collection trophies take the bulk of your time, and the performance trophies are a short but intense burst at the end.

## Rolling Tips That Make Trophy Hunting Faster

Control mastery is the single biggest multiplier on your platinum speed. These habits apply to every stage in the game.

| Habit | Why It Works | Best Used For |
|---|---|---|
| Roll in tight circles first | Builds a dense, stable core | Early-game size targets |
| Dash into clusters, not singles | Maximizes objects per second | Collection milestones |
| Sweep the stage perimeter | Rare items often sit at edges | Character and item hunting |
| Slow down in tight spaces | Prevents bouncing off walls | Indoor and corridor sections |
| Check the sky and rooftops | Vertical objects are easy to miss | Completionist trophies |
| Bank time early, relax late | Timer trophies punish slow starts | Performance challenges |

A dense core is the difference between a ball that handles like a dream and one that sheds objects on every corner. Spend the first thirty seconds of any run rolling in a spiral rather than sprinting toward the biggest object on screen.

### Learn the Dash, Then Abuse It

The dash or charge roll is usually the fastest way to close distance to a cluster you've already scouted. Pairing a dash with a well-timed turn lets you vacuum up an entire row of items in one motion. Practice this on a stage you've already cleared rather than during a timed run.

## Missable Trophies and Common Pitfalls

Missables are the reason most trophy hunters search for an Once Upon A Katamari trophy guide in the first place. Here are the patterns that catch people out.

| Pitfall | What Happens | How to Prevent It |
|---|---|---|
| Skipping optional side areas | Hidden characters stay hidden | Sweep every stage once with no goal |
| Rolling past unusual objects | Collection counters stall | Grab anything that looks out of place |
| Restarting stages too often | Wasted time, no cumulative progress | Finish runs even when they go badly |
| Assuming one playthrough is enough | Missing tiered trophies | Plan for at least one replay per stage |
| Ignoring menu stats | You lose track of what's left | Check the in-game records screen often |

The records or statistics screen in a Katamari game is your best friend. It usually tracks per-stage progress, item totals, and unlocked characters, which turns vague guessing into a precise checklist.

### Use the In-Game Records Screen as Your Tracker

Open it after every few stages. If a number hasn't moved, you know that stage isn't contributing to that trophy. This one habit removes almost all of the guesswork from cleanup.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are there missable trophies in Once Upon A Katamari?**

Yes — in most Katamari games, character unlocks, collection milestones, and one-off action trophies can be missed if you don't trigger or collect them during a stage. Because most of these are replayable through stage select, "missable" usually means "requires another run" rather than "permanently lost." Check your records screen regularly so nothing sneaks past you.

**How long does the platinum take?**

There's no fixed answer, because it depends heavily on how efficiently you route your replays. Community reports suggest the story and size trophies fall quickly, while the collection and performance tiers take the majority of the total time. A focused Once Upon A Katamari trophy guide approach — discovery run first, cleanup second — will always be faster than improvising.

**Do I need to beat the game on a hard difficulty?**

In most entries in the series, difficulty scaling is tied to stage objectives rather than a global setting. You'll typically need to clear every stage and hit each size target, which effectively means mastering the later, larger stages. Check the trophy descriptions for any explicit difficulty requirement before assuming.

**Can I earn trophies in co-op or with a second player?**

Multiplayer modes in Katamari games are usually separate from the campaign and often don't contribute to trophy progress. If you're hunting the platinum, assume solo play is required unless the trophy description specifically mentions multiplayer. Player experience suggests it's safer to grind everything solo and treat co-op as a bonus.

**What's the single best tip for this trophy list?**

Never leave a stage empty-handed. Even a failed run adds to cumulative counters, and those counters are what stand between you and the platinum. Roll everything, note everything unusual, and let the checklist do the rest.
