## How Long Does Once Upon a Katamari Take to Beat?

The King of All Cosmos has done it again: he and his royal family have accidentally obliterated Earth, the moon, and countless stars, leaving you to roll the cosmos back into shape. The short answer to **Once Upon A Katamari how long to beat** is about 7½ hours when you focus on the main objectives. Chase everything, though, and **Once Upon A Katamari how long to beat** stretches to roughly 21½ hours for full completion.

That roughly threefold gap is the whole story here. Knowing which number applies to you changes how you schedule sessions, whether you replay stages for better scores, and whether you bother hunting every last collectible on your first pass. Player-reported data from [HowLongToBeat's Once Upon a Katamari page](https://howlongtobeat.com/game/171891) backs up both figures, and community reports put a relaxed "main story plus some extras" run at around 12 hours.

|---|---|---|

| Main story + extra content | ~12 hours | Story plus a healthy amount of side rolling |
| 100% completion | ~21½ hours | All stars, collectibles, and unlockables |

If you have ever bounced off a Katamari game because it felt repetitive, the good news is that this entry keeps things moving. ## What Affects Your Playtime

Several design choices shape how fast you rebuild the sky. Understanding them lets you shave hours off a run without skipping content.

- **Size thresholds.** Each stage has a minimum katamari size to pass. Overshooting the goal is optional, but undershooting forces a restart.
- **Time limits.** Most stages are timed, so wasted movement is directly punished. Rolling into a wall is lost time you never get back.
- **Object density and routing.** Dense clusters of small objects are the fastest path to early growth; sparse areas are traps.
- **Score chasing.** Going for high ranks means replaying stages, which is the single biggest multiplier on total playtime.
- **Collectibles and customization.** Character customization options and unlockables pull you into optional stages you might otherwise skip.
- **Difficulty of later eras.** Historic Japan and the prehistoric stages tend to punish sloppy routing more than the opening areas.

### Tips to Beat Once Upon a Katamari Faster

If your goal is to shorten **Once Upon A Katamari how long to beat**, these habits make the biggest difference.

1. **Never stop rolling.** Momentum is progress. Even a bad object adds size.
2. **Start small and dense.** Scoop up clusters of tiny objects before chasing big set pieces.
3. **Learn the size curve.** Each stage has a point where growth accelerates; hitting it early snowballs the rest of the run.
4. **Skip optional ranks on your first pass.** Come back for high scores after you know the layout.
5. **Bank your unlocks.** Save customization and collectible hunting for a second playthrough when stages feel familiar.
6. **Restart early, not late.** If you are behind the size curve at the halfway mark, a quick restart beats a doomed attempt.

Players who follow those six rules consistently report finishing the story in the 6–7 hour range on a repeat run, which is a meaningful saving over the tracked average.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to beat Once Upon A Katamari if I only do the story?**
About 7½ hours when you focus on the main objectives. That covers every era and the credits, but skips optional ranks and most collectibles.

**Is Once Upon A Katamari how long to beat different for 100% completion?**
Yes, substantially. Full completion runs around 21½ hours, roughly triple the main story, because you are replaying stages for high scores and hunting down every unlockable.

**Does playing on a harder setting change the playtime?**
It can. Tighter time limits and stricter size goals mean more restarts, and each failed attempt adds minutes. Community reports suggest a harder run can add a few hours compared to a relaxed one.

**Can I finish the game faster on a second playthrough?**
Almost always. Once you know the object layouts and the size curve of each era, a story run can drop into the 6–7 hour range, which is a solid improvement over a blind first attempt.

**Is it worth replaying for extra content?**
If you enjoy route optimization, yes. The extra stages, character customization, and new soundtrack give you reasons to return, and the replay loop is where the game's longevity really lives.
