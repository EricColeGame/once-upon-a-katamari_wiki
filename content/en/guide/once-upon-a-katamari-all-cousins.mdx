## What Counts as a Cousin in Once Upon A Katamari

Cousins are the extended family of the Prince: same rolling job, wildly different silhouettes. In previous Katamari titles they were unlockable playable characters. In this entry, they double as collectibles scattered through stages, which means you'll find them while you're already rolling, not on a separate menu screen.

Each one behaves like a normal rollable object until you touch it, at which point it joins your collection and gets added to the family roster. Because they're small and often tucked near landmarks, walls, or set dressing, they're easy to roll straight past on a first playthrough.

If you want the publisher's own overview of the game before you start hunting, the [official Bandai Namco Once Upon A Katamari page](https://www.bandainamcoent.com/games/once-upon-a-katamari) is a solid starting point for platform and feature details.

A few things worth knowing up front:

- Cousins count toward the same completion percentage as Crowns and Presents.
- Some cousins only appear after you've met a specific stage condition.
- A handful are tied to late-game areas, so early 100% runs are impossible by design.

## The Full Collectible Breakdown

According to the widely referenced collectible guide published by PowerPyx, Once Upon A Katamari contains 248 total collectibles. Cousins make up a big chunk of that number, and they're the category most players finish last.

| Collectible Type | Total Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Crowns | 141 | Largest single category; spread across nearly every stage |
| Cousins | 68 | Required for two level unlocks and related trophies |
| Presents | 39 | Often tucked in corners and on rooftops |
| **Total** | **248** | Full completion target for 100% runs |

Walkthrough documentation on Neoseeker is blunt about why cousins matter: collecting all of them is what unlocks two levels later in the game, and it's necessary if you want every trophy or achievement. In other words, the cousin hunt isn't a side activity — it's a progression gate.

That has a practical consequence for how you play. Instead of treating cousins as a cleanup task after you've beaten the story, it's smarter to grab every one you spot on your first pass through a stage, then return only for the stragglers.

## How Multi-Cousin Stages Actually Work

This is the mechanic that trips up the most players. In stages that contain more than one cousin, you can't simply scoop up both in a single run. Player experience shared on the Katamari on the Web forums is consistent on this point: you must first clear the stage with the previous cousin rolled up before the next one becomes available.

In practice, that turns certain levels into repeat visits rather than one-and-done clears. The sequence below is the reliable way to handle them.

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify whether the stage holds one cousin or several | Determines whether you need multiple runs |
| 2 | Finish the stage with the current cousin collected | The clear is what triggers the next spawn |
| 3 | Replay the stage and check the previous cousin's location | The next cousin often appears elsewhere |
| 4 | Repeat until the stage stops producing new cousins | Signals the stage is fully harvested |
| 5 | Log the cousin in your own tracker | Prevents duplicate runs later |

Two habits make this much smoother. First, always finish a stage you've collected a cousin in — bailing out early wastes the trigger. Second, screenshot or note the roster screen before you quit a session, so you know exactly which stage needs another visit.

It's also worth knowing that the size requirement of a stage can matter. If a cousin sits behind a large object or on a high ledge, you may need to hit a certain katamari diameter before you can reach that area at all. Rolling a bigger ball first, then doubling back, is often faster than trying to squeeze in at minimum size.

## FAQ

**How many cousins are in Once Upon A Katamari?**

There are 68 collectible cousins, part of a 248-item collectible total that also includes 141 Crowns and 39 Presents. That 68 figure is specific to this game and shouldn't be confused with the larger playable roster across the whole Katamari series.

**Do I really need Once Upon A Katamari all cousins for the platinum trophy?**

Yes. Collecting the full cousin set unlocks two levels later in the game and is required for the related trophies and achievements. Skipping the hunt means leaving those unlocks and any completion-based rewards on the table.

**Why won't the next cousin show up in a stage that has several?**

Because the game gates them sequentially. In stages with multiple cousins, you have to clear the stage with the previous cousin already rolled up before the next one appears. Finish the run, then replay it.

**Are the 11 new cousins harder to find than the returning ones?**

Community reports suggest the newcomers are linked to the Prince Ship's time-travel mechanic, which usually means they sit in themed stages you visit later in the story. They're not necessarily harder — just later. Working through stages in story order keeps you from missing them entirely.
