Once Upon A Katamari looks simple from the outside: roll a sticky ball through history, pick up everything in your path, and watch it snowball into a planet-sized mess. In practice, the seventh entry in the Katamari series hides a surprising amount of structure under that chaos — which is exactly why a good Once Upon A Katamari walkthrough matters. The Prince travels through 10 stages called Eras, most of them set in their own time period, each with its own size targets and hidden collectibles.

Miss a Cousin or a Crown on your first pass and you will be replaying stages long after the credits roll. This Once Upon A Katamari walkthrough breaks the whole journey into pieces you can actually plan around, from your first sixty seconds in a new Era to the last Present on your checklist.

## How Once Upon A Katamari Works: Eras, Size Gates, and Time Limits

The core loop has not changed since the original game. Your katamari only picks up objects that are small enough relative to its current diameter, so every Era is built as a ladder: benches first, then people, then cars, then buildings. Grow past a threshold and an entirely new tier of junk becomes fair game.

Each Era hands you a target size and a countdown. Reach the target before the timer empties and the King of All Cosmos is satisfied. Fall short and you are sent back to try again — often with a lecture.

| Element | What it means | Walkthrough takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| Era selection | 10 stages, with nine set in distinct time periods | Treat each Era as its own checklist |
| Size target | The Prince must hit a required diameter | Chase the biggest objects you can currently grab |
| Time limit | Most rolls are measured in a few minutes | Plan your opening 60 seconds before you press start |
| Object tiers | Only items below your current size will stick | Revisit small, dense clusters after you grow |
| Hidden collectibles | Crowns, Cousins, and Presents | Sweep edges, corners, and interiors — not just open streets |

Time is the real enemy here. Community reports consistently point to the same pattern: players who hit the largest katamari sizes are the ones who spend their first minute rolling tight, dense clusters instead of wandering toward a distant landmark that looks tempting.

## Once Upon A Katamari Walkthrough: Era-by-Era Strategy

The most reliable structure for this Once Upon A Katamari walkthrough is a two-pass approach. On your first visit to an Era, ignore collectibles entirely and chase size. On the second pass, come back with a clean checklist and no pressure to grow.

That split works because collectible hunting and size chasing pull in opposite directions. Collectibles hide in corners, back alleys, and interiors — exactly the places where object density is low and your katamari grows slowly. Size chasing wants open plazas and dense clusters. Trying to do both at once usually means doing both badly.

- **Open with density.** Roll the tightest cluster of small objects you can see. Early growth compounds, and a bigger katamari unlocks bigger prizes.
- **Then expand outward.** Once you clear a zone, move to the next instead of grinding the same block.
- **Save the biggest objects for last.** Late growth is the fastest growth, so leave cars and buildings for the final stretch.
- **Learn the dash and quick-turn.** Mastering your movement tools is worth more than memorizing any single route.
- **Watch the camera.** Zoomed-out views help you spot clusters; zoomed-in views help you thread narrow alleys.

| Phase of a roll | Focus | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| First 60 seconds | Small, dense clusters | Fast early growth compounds for the rest of the run |
| Mid-run | Push into new zones | Fresh areas contain object tiers you could not grab before |
| Final 60 seconds | Largest available objects | Late growth gives the biggest size jumps per second |
| Cleanup run | Collectibles only | No size pressure means you can search slowly and thoroughly |

Player experience suggests that the tenth stage functions as a capstone of everything you have learned, so treat it as a victory lap only after your earlier Eras are cleaned up.

## Crowns, Cousins, and Presents: The Collectible Hunt

Once Upon A Katamari contains 248 collectible locations spread across three types: 141 Crowns, 68 Cousins, and 39 Presents. Crowns and Cousins are the ones that gate completion, while Presents unlock cosmetic items you can dress the Prince and his relatives in.

| Collectible | Count | Why it matters | Best approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crowns | 141 | Completion progress and unlocks | Sweep every Era systematically, one zone at a time |
| Cousins | 68 | Roster unlocks and trophies | Check rooftops, small rooms, and corners |
| Presents | 39 | Costumes and accessories | Many sit behind size gates, so return once you are huge |
| Drinks | — | Tied to a separate trophy challenge | Route through dense clusters of drink objects |

One note on the Cousin count: the widely referenced collectible guide lists 68, while some community reports mention 69. If your in-game counter refuses to hit 100 percent, double-check your list against a current, updated source rather than assuming you missed something obvious.

Cousins are the most common source of frustration, because they tend to hide where your katamari cannot easily reach at a small size. A practical tip for this Once Upon A Katamari walkthrough: finish the size objective first, then circle back. A giant katamari can roll straight over obstacles that block a small one.

## Trophies and Achievements: The Drinks Challenge and More

Trophy hunting is where the collectible grind pays off. Most of the list is straightforward — complete Eras, hit size milestones, and gather Crowns, Cousins, and Presents — but one challenge stands out. According to the trophy guide, you need to roll up as many drinks as you can within a seven-minute time limit in a dedicated stage. Drinks are a new collectible type in this entry, and they are scattered in clusters rather than spread evenly.

| Trophy category | What it typically demands | Prep tip |
|---|---|---|
| Collectible trophies | Crowns, Cousins, and Presents | Do a dedicated cleanup pass per Era |
| Time-limited rolls | Maximum pickups in a fixed window | Memorize a dense route before you start |
| Size milestones | Reaching large katamari diameters | Prioritize density over distance |
| Completion trophies | Finishing every Era | Save the toughest stage for last |

Exact trophy names differ between the PlayStation and Steam versions, so check the list on your platform. The structure, however, stays the same: a handful of skill-based challenges wrapped around one very long collectible hunt.

## Common Mistakes That Slow Down a Once Upon A Katamari Walkthrough

Even experienced rollers trip over the same handful of habits. Here are the ones that cost the most time, along with fixes that actually work.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Chasing collectibles on a timed first run | You grow too slowly and miss the size target | Split your runs into size passes and cleanup passes |
| Rolling the same block repeatedly | Diminishing returns once objects are cleared | Move to a fresh zone every 30–45 seconds |
| Ignoring small objects after growing | Wasted seconds on low-value pickups | Focus only on your current top tier |
| Skipping the dash and quick-turn | Slower routes, missed clusters | Practice movement in an easy Era first |
| Assuming you missed a collectible | Counts are sometimes misreported | Verify against a current guide before replaying |

The biggest one is impatience. It is tempting to grab that shiny Crown sitting just off your route, but a detour in the first minute of a timed roll can cost you the size target entirely. Grab it on the second pass.

## FAQ

**How long does a full Once Upon A Katamari walkthrough take?**
A casual playthrough of the 10 Eras takes a handful of hours, but a completion-focused Once Upon A Katamari walkthrough with all 248 collectibles will take considerably longer — most of the extra time goes into the Cousin hunt and the seven-minute drinks challenge.

**Do I need to collect everything in a single run of an Era?**
No. The two-pass method is far more efficient. Clear the size objective first, then return for Crowns, Cousins, and Presents with no timer pressure.

**Which collectible type is the hardest to finish?**
Cousins. They hide in small rooms, rooftops, and corners that a small katamari cannot reach, so many players finish their last few Cousins only after revisiting Eras with a much bigger ball.

**Can I replay Eras after completing them?**
Yes, and you should. Replays are the backbone of any cleanup strategy, since a maxed-out katamari makes short work of areas that were blocked off during your first visit.
